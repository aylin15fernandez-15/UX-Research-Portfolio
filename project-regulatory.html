Discovery Research · Ongoing · JPMorgan Chase

Firm-wide regulatory change research across lines of business

Understanding how legal and regulatory change management actually works across a global financial institution, spanning every major line of business and corporate function, requires access, patience, and the ability to operate in rooms where decisions get made at the highest levels.

Scale

21 senior leaders surveyed

Methods

Survey research · Discovery · Executive facilitation support

Timeline

February 2026 – ongoing

My role

Research support · Survey design · Offsite participation

Background

The scope of the problem

Legal and regulatory change, including new laws, updated rules, and shifting compliance requirements, affects every part of a firm like JPMorgan Chase. But how those changes are identified, assessed, implemented, and governed varies significantly across the firm's many lines of business and corporate functions.

A cross-functional working group was formed to understand that variation: how does regulatory change management actually work at the firm level, what are the pain points, and where do the biggest gaps in process, tooling, and governance live?

I was brought in to support the research effort, working alongside senior leaders who had been engaging with this problem from the inside.

Approach

How the research has been structured

The working group meets weekly, and research has run in parallel with those conversations. The primary research instrument was a discovery survey distributed to 21 senior leaders, each representing a different line of business or corporate function, designed to surface how regulatory change management was experienced across the full lifecycle:

  • Front end: how teams identify and assess the impact of incoming regulatory changes
  • Back end: how changes are implemented once their impact is understood
  • Governance layer: how oversight, accountability, and documentation function across the process

A specific focus of the research has been on forums and meetings, including how decisions are made, who is in the room, and how information flows between working groups, committees, and the functions responsible for execution.

Access

Working at the executive level

One of the most distinctive aspects of this project is the level of access it has required. Beyond the survey research, I have attended four days of executive offsites, across two separate two-day sessions, where senior leaders from across the firm gathered to work through structural decisions about how regulatory change management should operate going forward.

Being in those rooms, at that level, as a researcher is unusual at any career stage. It has required the ability to observe carefully, synthesize quickly, and support decision-making conversations without inserting the research process where it doesn't belong.

The offsites have been as much about understanding how decisions get made as about the content of the decisions themselves, which has informed how the research is framed and what questions remain most important to answer.

Status

Where this stands

This project is ongoing. The working group continues to meet weekly, and the research is evolving alongside the conversations it is meant to inform. Survey findings have shaped the agenda and focus of subsequent sessions, and the research is actively influencing how leaders think about process standardization, governance structure, and tooling decisions at the firm level.

It is, in many ways, the most complex research context I've worked in, and the one where the stakes of getting it right are highest.
