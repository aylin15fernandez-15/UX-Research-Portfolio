Heuristic Evaluation · JPMorgan Chase

Finding usability gaps in a regulatory change management tool

Product owners of an internal tool used to manage legal and regulatory changes needed an outside expert perspective before locking in their roadmap. A structured heuristic evaluation surfaced prioritized usability issues that gave the team a clear direction forward.

Scale

Solo expert review

Methods

Heuristic evaluation · Expert review · Written report

Timeline

2025

My role

Sole evaluator · Report author · Findings presentation

Background

The ask

An internal tool used by the risk management and compliance space at JPMorgan Chase, designed to process and track legal and regulatory changes such as laws, rules, regulations, and updates to legal entities, was undergoing roadmap planning. The product owners wanted an expert usability review before finalizing their priorities.

They came to our UX team with a straightforward request: take a fresh look at the tool and tell us what you see.

Approach

How I ran the evaluation

I conducted an independent heuristic evaluation of the tool, working through each major workflow and surface against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics. For each issue identified, I documented:

  • The heuristic violated and a description of the issue
  • The specific location in the tool where it appeared
  • A severity rating to help the team prioritize
  • A recommended change or design direction to address it

The evaluation covered the tool's core workflows: intake, tracking, status management, and reporting outputs. Issues ranged from navigation inconsistencies and unclear labeling to missing feedback states and workflow logic that didn't match how users thought about their work.

Delivery

What was delivered

Findings were compiled into a structured written report organized by severity, allowing the product team to immediately see which issues warranted urgent attention versus longer-term consideration. Each finding was written to be actionable: not just "this is a problem" but "here's what we'd recommend instead and why."

The product team found the evaluation directly useful for roadmap planning. Rather than debating what to prioritize, they had a structured, evidence-based list to work from, organized by impact and effort in a way that mapped to how they made decisions.

Impact

Outcome

The findings were adopted into the product roadmap. The evaluation gave the team external validation of issues they had suspected and surfaced others they hadn't seen, both of which fed directly into how they sequenced upcoming work.

It also established a working relationship between the product team and our UX practice, opening the door to future research collaboration as the tool continued to evolve.
