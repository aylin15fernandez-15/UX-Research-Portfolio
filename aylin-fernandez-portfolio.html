About

Research that moves
organizations forward.

I came to UX research through an unconventional path. I studied psychology and Spanish at Texas State University, then entered a PhD program in developmental psychology at UMass Amherst — where I spent three years doing rigorous academic research on stress and wellbeing in adolescents of color, including biological data collection like cortisol and HRV. I presented at conferences, wrote extensively, and developed a deep foundation in research methodology — all through the Family Relationships, Affective Science, and Minority Health (FAM) Lab.

What I realized is that I loved the research — the investigation, the concrete findings, the act of making sense of complex human behavior. What I didn't love was where academic research ended: publications and grant proposals, rather than decisions and change. I wanted my findings to move something.

That realization led me to UX research. I interned at JPMorgan Chase in the summer of 2025, working within Regulatory Controls & Operational Risk Technology under Corporate Technology — and the work clicked immediately. I took a full-time role and haven't looked back. I bring the rigor of academic research into an industry context, with a particular interest in complex organizational systems, internal tooling, and the humans navigating high-stakes processes.

ResearchOps · Quantitative

Building a CSAT baseline across 9 internal tools at JPMorgan Chase

Regulatory Controls & Operational Risk Technology had no standardized way to measure product satisfaction. I designed and launched a CSAT program across the team's full product suite — establishing a repeatable measurement framework from scratch. Surveys captured satisfaction, ease of use, and a product-specific third variable on a 5-point Likert scale, supplemented by open-ended qualitative questions to contextualize scores. Findings were delivered as one-page infographic readouts to product stakeholders, including score interpretation, user role breakdowns, and response rates. A key part of the work was stakeholder management: most baseline scores fell in the "needing improvement" or "fair" range, and reframing these results as a starting point — not a verdict — was critical to keeping product owners engaged and aligned on next steps.

Methods Survey design, Likert scale, qualitative analysis, stakeholder readouts
Scale 300+ participants · 9 products · ongoing
Discovery Research

Reshaping the direction of an analytics platform through discovery at JPMorgan Chase

Senior leaders were weighing whether to rebuild a previously failed internal reporting platform for regulatory and operational risk work. Before any build decisions were made, I led discovery research to understand how reporting actually happened across the organization. Through 11 interviews with end users, product stakeholders, and governance leads, we mapped a fragmented, manual process: report requests passed through multiple hands before reaching an engineer who would pull and massage raw data in Tableau, then send results back upstream — making teams reactive rather than analytical. A key finding was that reporting maturity varied significantly across sub-teams, many of whom already had lightweight systems in place. Rebuilding a single platform and forcing migration would have created massive adoption risk. The research reoriented the product direction entirely: instead of a standalone tool, the team built a modular data and feature layer that sub-teams could opt into from within their existing tools — a lower-lift, higher-adoption solution that directly addressed the pain points we surfaced.

Methods Discovery interviews, workflow mapping, stakeholder readout
Impact 11 interviews · redirected product strategy
Heuristic Evaluation

Identifying usability gaps in a regulatory change management tool at JPMorgan Chase

Product owners of an internal tool used to process legal and regulatory changes — including laws, rules, regulations, and legal entity updates within the risk management and compliance space — requested a heuristic evaluation to inform their product roadmap. I independently evaluated the tool against established usability heuristics, documented findings, and delivered a written report with prioritized recommendations. The product team found the evaluation directly actionable and incorporated the insights into their roadmap planning.

Methods Heuristic evaluation, expert review
Impact Findings adopted into product roadmap
Discovery · Ongoing

Researching firm-wide regulatory change processes across lines of business at JPMorgan Chase

As part of a cross-functional working group meeting weekly since February 2026, I've been supporting senior leaders in understanding how legal and regulatory change management operates across the entire firm — spanning multiple lines of business and corporate functions. Discovery surveys with 21 senior participants helped map pain points and process differences across the full regulatory lifecycle: from front-end impact assessment through to implementation and governance oversight. Research has focused on how forums and decision-making meetings function at the firm-wide level. I've attended four days of executive offsites, providing on-the-ground research support as senior leaders work through structural decisions — an unusual level of access that has shaped both the research questions and the depth of findings.

Methods Survey research, discovery, executive facilitation support
Scale 21 senior participants · firm-wide scope · ongoing
Consumer Research · Independent

Clarifying product messaging for Ollin, an ancient grain cake mix brand

My sister is the founder of Ollin — a cake box mix brand built around ancient grains. As the product approached its first year, consumer feedback suggested people were misreading the packaging as gluten-free, when in fact the brand's message is the opposite: a celebration of ancient, gluten-positive grains. She asked me to run a structured market research survey to test whether that perception was real and where it was coming from. Using 1Q, a market research platform, I surveyed 50 adults across the US, showing them photos of the packaging and asking about their interpretations. The findings confirmed the misread was happening — and more importantly, identified which specific design elements were driving it. I delivered the findings with design-specific recommendations, which my sister brought to her designer. The packaging was updated as a result, sharpening the brand's messaging and better communicating what makes Ollin distinct.

Methods Survey research, perception testing, consumer insights
Scale 50 participants · led to packaging redesign

How I work

My research process

I believe good research is as much about organizational trust as it is about methodology. In large institutions, the hardest part often isn't running the study — it's getting the right people in the room, and getting findings to actually change something.

My process is built around rigor, transparency, and making findings impossible to ignore.

01

Scope

Align on the right question. Understand stakeholder assumptions. Define what a useful answer looks like before a single session is scheduled.

02

Design

Choose methods to match the question — not the other way around. Build guides, screeners, and stimuli that get at behavior, not opinion.

03

Field

Run sessions that create space for people to surprise me. Recruit for diversity of perspective, not demographic checkbox.

04

Activate

Deliver findings in formats that create action. A great insight that doesn't move a team forward is just an interesting document.
