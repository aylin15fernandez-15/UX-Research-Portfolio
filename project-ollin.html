Consumer Research · Independent Consulting

Packaging perception testing for Ollin, an ancient grain cake mix

Ollin is a cake mix brand built around the celebration of ancient grains, a product intentionally and enthusiastically gluten-positive. But consumers kept reading the packaging as gluten-free. A structured perception study helped identify exactly what was causing the misread and gave the brand a clear path to fixing it.

Scale
50 participants
Methods
Survey research · Consumer perception testing · 1Q platform
Timeline
Summer 2025
My role
Sole researcher · Survey design · Findings delivery · Design recommendations
Background

The brand and the problem

Ollin is an independent cake mix brand built around the use of ancient grains. The brand's central message is a celebration of what ancient grains bring to baking, like flavor, nutrition, and texture, and the packaging was designed to communicate that distinctiveness.

But as the brand approached its first year, a pattern had emerged in consumer feedback: people were encountering the product and thinking it was gluten-free. Not just occasionally, but frequently enough that it was becoming a real concern. For a brand that is explicitly the opposite of gluten-free, this was a significant messaging problem.

The hypothesis was that either A) something about the packaging design was triggering a "gluten-free" association, or B) people associate 'healthy-looking' product variations as gluten-free. The question became: Is this A) an issue with the current packaging design, or B) a result of the gluten-free wave. Before suggesting any design changes, I explored these research questions.

Approach

Designing the study

I designed a consumer perception survey using 1Q, a market research platform that enables targeted recruitment of specific consumer profiles. The sample was defined as adults in the United States, a broad but appropriate screen for a consumer packaged goods product available nationally.

The survey showed participants photos of the Ollin packaging and asked a series of questions designed to surface:

  • Their initial impression of the product, including what kind of product it was and who it seemed designed for
  • What they believed the product's key characteristics were, including any dietary attributes they associated with it
  • Which specific elements of the packaging shaped those impressions
  • Open-ended space for anything else they noticed or interpreted

50 responses were collected, a meaningful sample for a consumer perception question of this specificity.

Findings

What the data showed

The survey highlighted findings that went against our hypothesis that the packaging design or 'healthy' visual cues were causing consumers to mistake the product as gluten-free. With 70% of participants recognizing the presence of gluten and 17/50 defining the target market as 'gluten-tolerant,' it became clear that gluten misperception was not the issue.

Instead, the primary visual communication gap was in the ingredient story: 30% of participants did not associate the product with ancient or traditionally-processed grains, potentially causing consumers to view it as a standard, conventional cake mix rather than a premium ancient-grain offering.

With gluten perception firmly established, the immediate design opportunity centered on closing the 30% perception gap around ancient grains through stronger packaging hierarchy.

Impact

From research to redesign

I delivered findings to the Ollin founder alongside specific, design-actionable recommendations, identifying which elements to reconsider and why, grounded in what participants had actually responded to.

The Ollin founder brought those findings to her brand designer, who combined the research insights with their own design perspective to work through the changes. The result was a packaging refresh that sharpened the visual communication of what Ollin actually is: a celebration of ancient, gluten-positive grains.
